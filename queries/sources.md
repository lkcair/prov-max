# Needs and their sources

| Need | Question | What a record must hold | Sources (quote, location) |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | Who sent which task to which agent, in what order, and with what instruction? | Dispatcher identity; receiving agent; the task text it received (objective, output format, tools allowed, boundaries); order or time of the dispatch. | MARS (Teach the orchestrator how to delegate): "Each subagent needs an objective, an output format, guidance on the tools and sources to use, and clear task boundaries." and "Without detailed task descriptions, agents duplicate work, leave gaps, or fail to find necessary information." WW p.2: ϕ(t) names the active agent at each step, "thus specifying the turn-based rule." GRADE p.4, coordination failure: control "moves wrongly, through a handoff sent to the wrong" agent. MAST p.9: AppWorld premature terminations are "potentially due to its star topology and lack of a predefined workflow making" termination conditions less obvious. |
| N2 | What did the router or planner decide, and what plan did it make? | The routing outcome (chosen agent, model tier, or category); the plan or decomposition as a recorded artifact; which executed steps came from which plan item. The plan-item to step link is inferred, no source. OTEL has a `plan` span with sibling task spans, and no attribute links a plan step to the span that runs it. | BEA (Routing): "Routing classifies an input and directs it to a specialized followup task." BEA (Summary): "Prioritize transparency by explicitly showing the agent’s planning steps." OTEL agent spans, Plan span: the spans produced "from the plan are typically sibling operations under the same" agent invocation. TCA p.4: each DAG node "is tagged with a model tier t and a memory strategy m before execution begins". CLAIM p.2: "model may propose; the control plane decides what authority actually exists." MARS: the lead agent saves its plan to memory because "it is important to retain the plan". |
| N3 | Was the planner's stated reason the real cause of the route? | Keep the stated rationale as its own artifact, with no causal edge from it. | HAN p.3: an agent's account of its own reasoning enters the graph "as an artifact with no causal edges". MAST FM 2.6 (p.17), reasoning-action mismatch, needs both the reasoning and the action recorded. Niche (two sources). |
| N4 | Who is responsible for this action, all the way back to the human or principal who started it? | A responsibility chain per action, from the immediate executor through every delegating agent to the originating principal, including any human approval step on the way. | AUD p.5: responsibility "is often a chain: user → agent → skill → tool → service, possibly with a human approval step in" between. AUD p.8: "runtime systems often capture the immediate executor more reliably than the full upstream delegation" chain. HAN p.5: accountability lifts to principals "along ‘actedOnBehalfOf’ chains, weighted by foreseeability" and control. SURV p.25: the record "should identify not only which final answer was wrong, but which agent introduced the unsupported claim, which agent" failed to verify it, and which message propagated it. |
| N5 | What did agent A know, what did it pass to B, and did B use it? (This separates withholding from ignoring from lost context.) | Per handoff: sender, receiver, role, message content, cited evidence, delegated task. Also the sender's own private observations, and what was in the receiver's context when it acted. | MAST p.7: FC2 diagnosis is hard because "surface behaviors (e.g., missing information) can stem from different root causes like withholding" (FM 2.4), ignoring input (FM 2.5), or context mismanagement (FM 1.4). SURV p.27 Table 10, inter-agent communication: "Sender, receiver, role, message content, cited evi-" dence, delegated task, and verification status. AT p.4: each action conditions on a history subset H_t, and "The structure of Ht is implementation-dependent." MARS (Appendix): "Subagents call tools to store their work in external systems, then pass lightweight references back to the coordinator." |
| N6 | Which agent was this, with what role, prompt, tools, and version, when it was created during the run? | A stable identity for each agent instance, including those spawned at run time; its creator; its role or system prompt; its tool set; its code, prompt, and model version. OTEL has a `create_agent` span only for remote agent services, and advises against in-memory ids, so agents spawned in process get a name and no id. | WW p.3: the run-time team builder works by "assigning appropriate agent names, prompts, and necessary" tools. MAST p.17, FM 1.2 (disobey role specification) needs each agent's role prompt. OTEL registry, `gen_ai.agent.id`: "It's NOT RECOMMENDED to record in-memory agent instance ids on this attribute due to their transient nature." OTEL registry: "Instrumentation MUST NOT generate `gen_ai.main_agent.id` from an in-memory or process-local identifier." MARS (Deployment): "whenever we deploy updates, agents might be anywhere in their process", so old and new versions run at once. |
| N7 | What did each step call (tool, API, retrieval, database), with what arguments, and what came back? | Per call: tool or source id, caller agent, arguments, result or error, time. A record that only says "tool X was called" is not enough. | AUD p.3: policy-relevant actions are tool invocations, "external requests, file operations, database queries, approvals, and delegation events." AUD p.23: a record can list every action and still fail when it notes the "tool was called, omitting arguments, output, caller identity, and approval context". SURV p.5: "tool traces record calls, arguments, outputs, permissions, and errors; memory traces expose cross-session" influence. OTEL `execute_tool` and `retrieval` spans: `gen_ai.tool.name`, `gen_ai.tool.call.id`, `gen_ai.data_source.id`, and arguments and result as opt-in ("The result returned by the tool call (if any and if execution was successful)."). MARS (Debugging): "Were the agents using bad search queries? Choosing poor sources? Hitting tool failures?" |
| N8 | Where did each argument of a consequential call come from, and did untrusted content shape it? | Per argument: the message, document, tool output, or memory item it was taken from, plus a trust label on that source. | SURV p.8: "Trustworthy tool use therefore requires tracing why a tool was selected, where its arguments came from, whether its" output was reliable. SURV p.9: the "key question is whether malicious content influenced a later tool call, argument value, memory update, or external" action. AUD p.4, a data-flow policy: "no external network call may follow access to data class Y without sanitization." HAN p.3: provenance labels are set "at the witness boundary at ingestion and immutable thereafter". |
| N9 | What did each step rely on? | Input edges per step (what it read or used), each marked as observed in the trace, declared by instrumentation, or inferred. | GRADE p.1: traces record which agent acted and in what order, "never what it relied on, the state it read, and the" state it wrote. GRADE p.7: "Who&When records no per-step read or write," so dependency must be inferred there. GRADE p.3: its model "stamps each dependency edge observed, declared," or inferred. SURV p.6: "logs answer what happened; execution graphs answer what depended on what." |
| N10 | What did the run change in the outside world, and did the change the agent claimed really happen? | Per write or side effect: target resource; its version or state before and after; the agent; the authorization it held; a record written before the effect. Also the agent's claim, kept apart from the observed effect. | MAST p.46 (FM 3.1 example): "What happened: The editor claimed an edit that was not made." CLAIM p.5: every request revalidates intent and base commit, and "A durable prepare record is written before the filesystem effect." GRADE p.3: resource nodes are the state a decision reads or writes, versioned in time. SURV p.17: a full trace must record "external state changes, and policy or permission context". MARS (Appendix): with stateful agents, "each action can change the environment for subsequent steps". BEA (Appendix 1): "Actions such as issuing refunds or updating tickets can be handled programmatically". |
| N11 | A source, write, or memory turned out bad. What did it affect downstream? (impact) | Forward edges from every data item to the steps, claims, writes, and answers that used it, so the affected set can be computed and marked stale. | SURV p.26: the system "should invalidate the memory and identify downstream claims or actions that depended on it." CLAIM p.4: when a producer changes, "staleness propagates transitively to downstream consumers until affected work is amended and re-" admitted. GRADE p.12: "blast hub share (the most-relied-upon node’s share" of downstream steps). SURV p.18: "Future systems should use provenance to invalidate stale memory, quarantine contaminated evidence, retry" calls. |
| N12 | Was the data the agent read already out of date? | Version, timestamp, or last-modified date of each retrieved item. | AT p.9 (case study): the root cause is a stale file fetched at step 2, visible only from "The log entry showing last edit: 2021-08-19". GRADE p.4: reliance failure when a "step used has gone bad: a value read early and now" stale. OTEL gap: retrieval records document ids and scores, with no version. |
| N13 | Was this final action a clean execution, or a retry or fallback that went around a check? | Each attempt as its own identifiable step: an attempt index, the reason for the retry, the outcome. Phase-entry and phase-exit markers, so "did not happen" can be told apart from "not recorded". OTEL folds automatic retries into one logical span, which loses this. | AUD p.4: retries, fallbacks, approvals, and handoffs "are rarely represented as identifiable" phases, so the auditor cannot tell whether "the final action was a clean execution or a policy-circumventing retry." AUD p.4: this would "require explicit phase-entry and phase-exit markers." OTEL model spans: a retried request's "corresponding span SHOULD cover the duration of the logical operation with all" retries. AT p.9: a patch-and-rerun loop hid the root cause (the agent "treated the incorrect column name as a simple typo to be fixed"). |
| N14 | Did the loop repeat finished work, and why did it stop (or fail to stop)? | An ordered step sequence with content, so repeats can be matched; the stop rule (max iterations, verifier sign-off, sentinel); who ended the loop and why. | MAST p.17, FM 1.3: "Unnecessary reiteration of previously completed steps". MAST p.17, FM 1.5: "Lack of recognition or understanding of the" termination criteria. MAST p.27: a cyclic graph "with a maximum iteration cutoff to prevent infinite loops", and "we ensured that only superior agents can finalize conversations". BEA (Agents): "it’s also common to include stopping conditions (such as a maximum number of iterations) to maintain control." MARS: early failure modes included "agents continuing when they already had sufficient results". |
| N15 | In an evaluator-optimizer or reflection cycle, which critique judged which draft, and which revision did it cause? | The draft; the evaluator's verdict and feedback; the link from the feedback to the next draft; feedback written into memory. | BEA (Evaluator-optimizer): "one LLM call generates a response while another provides evaluation and feedback in a loop." OTEL events, `gen_ai.evaluation.result`: "This event SHOULD be parented to GenAI operation span being evaluated when possible or set `gen_ai.response.id` when span id is not available." SURV p.12: "Reflexion writes verbal feedback into episodic memory after failures". OTEL has no link from feedback to the revised draft (inferred gap). |
| N16 | Which branches ran in parallel from which parent, and which branch outputs did the aggregator use? | A fan-out record (parent step, its branches, the subtask each received); a fan-in record (the aggregating step and each branch output it used). Recording outputs the aggregator dropped is inferred, no source. | BEA (Parallelization): "LLMs can sometimes work simultaneously on a task and have their outputs aggregated programmatically." MARS: the lead agent "spins up 3-5 subagents in parallel", and vague delegation made subagents duplicate work. MAST p.20: HyperAgent child agents "process tasks asynchronously, enabling parallel execution". TCA p.3: in a fan-in graph, context growth is bounded "by the maximum fan-in along the critical path." OTEL gap: the word "parallel" appears in none of the docs, and no fan-out, join, or link rule exists. |
| N17 | In voting or self-consistency, what were the candidates, the rule, and the result? | Each candidate output; the vote rule or threshold; the tally; the selected answer. Tally and rule are inferred, no source states them as record fields. | BEA (Parallelization): "Voting: Running the same task multiple times to get diverse outputs." and votes may use "different vote thresholds". MAST p.25: a tactic is "multiple LLM calls with majority voting or resampling until verification". OTEL registry, `gen_ai.request.choice.count`: "The target number of candidate completions to return." Niche. |
| N18 | Was harm spread across interchangeable parallel agents so that no single one is a but-for cause? | The set of redundant instances upstream of an action, and replay results over them. | HAN p.1: "attribution laundering, i.e., spreading an act across" redundant agents until none is a but-for cause. HAN p.4: a "consequential action spans interchangeable instances such that" no single node is a cut vertex. Niche (one source). |
| N19 | In what order did asynchronous agents really act and exchange messages, across processes and queues? | Per channel: a monotonic sequence number and timestamp per message; a trace or correlation id carried across process and protocol boundaries; queue and completion status of background work. | MARS (Synchronous execution): asynchronous execution "adds challenges in result coordination, state consistency, and error propagation across the subagents." HAN p.3: each witness assigns "a monotonic per-channel sequence number and hash-chains its" entries. OTEL MCP doc: the propagated context in "`params._meta` as a parent for MCP server span and SHOULD link current ambient" context. OTEL `gen_ai.response.status` has `queued` and `in_progress`. MAST p.20: HyperAgent agents work from queues. |
| N20 | Two agents changed the same shared state at once. Which change is current, and was the other superseded, rejected, or lost? | The version each writer read before writing; each attempted write; which was admitted, rejected, or superseded, and by what rule; the writer's lease or fencing token, so a stale writer can be recognized. | CLAIM p.1: parallel agents interfere by "expanding beyond planned scope, or relying on premises invalidated by concurrent work." CLAIM p.3: "Unknown overlapping writes fail" closed. CLAIM p.5: each writer "lease receives a larger monotonic fencing token." GSM p.11: "concurrent writes race at the dedup gate", and p.12 a contradictory write can be rejected "before the asynchronous detector ever sees it." |
| N21 | Did shared state carry one user's or tenant's data into another's run? | Owner and visibility scope of every shared item; the reader's resolved identity; the access decision. | AUD p.11: through a shared state layer, "scope-bound artifacts from one user can be reapplied to another with no attacker involved". GSM p.4 to 5: unauthorized leakage is one of four fleet-memory failure modes, and every retrieval path must check scope. |
| N22 | What interaction patterns does the system show across many runs (emergent behavior, odd structures)? | Run records with comparable structure (agent, step type, edges), so patterns can be counted across runs without reading content. | MARS: "Multi-agent systems have emergent behaviors, which arise without specific programming." MARS (Debugging): "we monitor agent decision patterns and interaction structures, all without monitoring the contents of individual conversations, to maintain user privacy." GRADE p.8: scan fleet "runs for anomalous dependency patterns without" reading content. WW p.8 (Finding 6): methods find the most failure-prone agent across many runs more reliably than in a single run. |
| N23 | Where did this memory come from, who wrote it, and from what? | Per memory write: writer agent; source; timestamp; evidence it was based on; derivation parent; scope; confidence. OTEL gap: the memory span has no agent or conversation attribute, so it does not say which agent wrote. | SURV p.12: "Each memory write should carry source and lineage metadata, including source" type, timestamp, authoring agent, supporting evidence, transformation operation, confidence, and update history. GSM p.5: "Every memory object stores its writer identity, source system," derivation history, and modification lineage. GSM p.5, provenance collapse: "Retrieved memories cannot be traced to their origin." HAN p.3: memory write is witnessed choke point W4. OTEL model spans, Memory: "Describes a memory operation, including memory store lifecycle operations and memory record operations." |
| N24 | Which memories did each step read, and why? | Per read: query, items returned with their versions, ranking, the consuming step. | SURV p.27 Table 10, memory retrieval: retrieval query, retrieved items, ranking score, context of reuse, downstream consumer. OTEL `search_memory` with `gen_ai.memory.query.text` and `gen_ai.memory.records` (opt-in). TCA p.3: per node, the injected memory token count, recorded "alongside the tier and memory strategy in use." |
| N25 | Was the memory read stale, superseded, or contradicted when it was used? | Per memory item: previous version; supersedes link; status (active or outdated); contradiction markers; time of each change. Reads must point at the version they got. | GSM p.5, stale propagation: "another agent continues retrieving outdated state." GSM p.11: a "newer row carrying supersedes_id pointing at the older," and the older is flipped to outdated. SURV p.13: the record must say whether "later observations superseded it." SURV p.27 Table 10, memory update: "Previous version, update source, update reason, con-" flict status, invalidation marker. |
| N26 | What was in each agent's working context at each step, and was something truncated or lost? | Per step: the context or history subset the agent saw; compaction or truncation events. | MAST p.17, FM 1.4: "interaction history and reverting to an antecedent conversational state". AT p.4: H_t is the subset of history each action conditioned on. OTEL `gen_ai.conversation.compacted`. MARS: context beyond 200,000 tokens is truncated, so the plan is saved to memory. |
| N27 | When did untrusted content become trusted memory, and can it be quarantined? | Trust label on the input of each memory write; any verification step between them; quarantine and invalidation events. | HAN p.4: "A memory write depending on an untrusted-labeled segment" "while citing no witnessed verification act is the moment con-" tent becomes trusted internal state. SURV p.13: with memory, "a single-run attack becomes a long-horizon provenance failure." AUD p.25: "A skill instructed the agent to modify MEMORY.md, a file that influences long-term agent behavior." |
| N28 | Which step failed, how, and with what error? | Per step: error type, message, timeout or rate-limit status, tool error flag, abnormal finish reason; the feedback the environment returned. | OTEL exceptions doc: an exception event for "API errors, rate limiting, model errors, timeouts, or other errors"; `error.type` on every span. AT p.5: the analyzer needs "environmental feedback F (e.g., code execution errors, tool errors), and the ground-truth solution G". MARS (Debugging): "Adding full production tracing let us diagnose why agents failed and fix issues systematically." |
| N29 | Which agent caused the failure, and at which step? (failure attribution) | Ordered steps with the acting agent on each; the content of each step; the user query; the final outcome; task and role specifications; ground truth when available. | WW p.3: the goal is "the failure-responsible agent and the" decisive error step, taking "the earliest error in time as the principal cause of failure." SURV p.27 Table 10, multi-agent failure: "Responsible agent, responsible step, error mode," propagated message, counterfactual trajectory. AT p.10: errors "are often subtle, originate early, and remain hidden behind seemingly correct outputs." WW p.7: "Our findings indicate that all three methods exhibit a decline" as logs get longer. MAST p.2: "the difficulty in verifying ground truth for root cause detection". |
| N30 | Can the run be replayed or resumed from the failing step to test a fix? | A frozen prefix up to step t: recorded tool responses, model and version, sampling parameters and seed, the state at t; checkpoints. | AT p.4: a corrected action is substituted and "all subsequent steps are re-simulated." HAN p.4: a determinism capsule holds seed "and sampling parameters, model version hashes and all tool" responses, so replay runs against a recorded environment. MARS: "Agents make dynamic decisions and are non-deterministic between runs, even with identical prompts." MARS: "Instead, we built systems that can resume from where the agent was when the errors occurred." SURV p.27 Table 9: "Checkpoints, rollback targets, invalidation events," repair steps, human approvals. |
| N31 | Is the record complete, so that a missing event means the event did not happen? | Every channel captured (calls, messages, memory writes, delegations); sequence numbers that expose gaps; a declared list of channels that are not captured. | MAST p.20: without full traces, "This lack of transparency into the internal conversational and operational steps" made annotation infeasible. HAN p.1: capture at choke points outside "the agents’ reach makes omissions detectable, not only tampering." HAN p.2: tamper-evident logs prove "integrity, never completeness". AUD p.4: phase markers needed to tell "did not occur" from "not recorded". |
| N32 | Which check ran on what, and what did it decide? | Per check: the check or policy id; the item it checked; the verdict (allow, block, pending, pass, fail); the reason; who or what ran it. A blocked action is recorded too. OTEL gap: no span for guardrails or approvals, and the evaluation event does not say whether a human or a model evaluated. | AUD p.8: "signed, timestamped record of the action, the policy evaluation, and the outcome." AUD p.13: "A blocked action still needs an explanation." SURV p.10: rules decide whether an action should proceed, be restricted, require "confirmation, or be blocked". MAST p.8: "sole reliance on final-stage, low-level checks is inadequate". BEA (Prompt chaining): "You can add programmatic checks (see "gate” in the diagram below) on any intermediate steps". |
| N33 | Who approved, overrode, or stopped this, and when? | Per human act: the person (or role), time, the item approved or overridden, the decision, and the steps that followed from it. | EU Art. 14(4), PDF pp.129 and 130 (printed 125 and 126): overseers must be able "to decide, in any particular situation, not to use the high-risk AI system or to otherwise disregard, override or reverse the output of" the system, and "to intervene in the operation of the high-risk AI system or interrupt" it. (This copy letters these points (f) and (g).) EU Art. 12(3)(d), PDF p.127: for Annex III 1(a) systems, logs must hold "the identification of the natural persons involved in the verification" of results. AUD p.13: a "human override still needs provenance." HAN p.4: interventions are themselves witnessed. BEA (Agents): "Agents can then pause for human feedback at checkpoints or when encountering blockers." SURV p.27 Table 9: "repair steps, human approvals, and state-change" records. |
| N34 | Can operators see risky situations and anomalies while the system runs and afterwards? | Automatic event logs over the system's lifetime that capture events tied to risk, substantial modification, and post-market monitoring; the period of each use (start and end). | EU Art. 12(1), PDF p.126 (printed 122): systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording" "of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system." Art. 12(2): logs must cover "identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI system" presenting a risk, and "facilitating the post-market monitoring referred to in Article 72". Art. 14(4), PDF p.129: overseers must monitor operation "including in view of detecting and addressing anomalies," dysfunctions and unexpected performance. Art. 12(3)(a), PDF p.127: "recording of the period of each use of the system (start date and" time), for Annex III 1(a) only. HAN p.4: "Indicators do not adjudicate; they gate." |
| N35 | How many tokens did each call and each agent use, and of which kind? | Per model call: input, output, cache read, cache write, and reasoning tokens, in billed units; model; the agent and invocation it belongs to, with sub-agent calls counted on the sub-agent. | OTEL registry, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`: the "value matches the units the customer is charged for." OTEL metrics, `gen_ai.invoke_agent.inference_calls`: calls by sub-agents are recorded against "those agents' own invocations so that each inference call is counted" once. MARS: "multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats", and "We found that token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance". BEA (Agents): "The autonomous nature of agents means higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors." |
| N36 | How much of each node's bill was context handed to it (memory, upstream outputs), and how much it generated itself? | Per node: base prompt tokens and injected tokens counted apart; output tokens; the model tier and its price; the memory strategy; depth in the workflow. | TCA p.1: observability tools report total token "cost but do not separate the tokens a node generates from the" tokens it is handed. TCA p.3: per-node counts are recorded at the prompt "assembly step, which records the per-node counts alongside" the tier and memory strategy. TCA p.3: a token count is "meaningful only alongside the tier that produced it". Niche (one source). |
| N37 | Where did the time go, and which agent held up the run? | Start and end time per workflow, agent invocation, tool call, and model call; time to first token; wait time on the slowest branch. | OTEL metrics: `gen_ai.invoke_workflow.duration`, `gen_ai.invoke_agent.duration`, `gen_ai.execute_tool.duration`, time to first chunk. MARS: "the entire system can be blocked while waiting for a single subagent to finish searching". GSM p.13: the write-to-visible delay of shared memory is a budget the orchestrator must plan against. |
| N38 | Did the system spend effort out of proportion to the task (too many agents or calls)? | Count of agents spawned, tool calls, and model calls per task. | MARS: "Early agents made errors like spawning 50 subagents for simple queries". OTEL metrics `gen_ai.invoke_agent.inference_calls` and `gen_ai.invoke_agent.tool_calls`. |
| N39 | Does the record hold more personal or secret content than it needs? | Content (prompts, messages, tool arguments and results, memory text) off by default or kept outside the record behind references; structure and ids kept; redaction and minimization rules recorded. | OTEL model spans, Full (buffered) content: "OpenTelemetry instrumentations SHOULD NOT capture them by default, but SHOULD" allow opt-in, and "Recording large or sensitive content in telemetry may be problematic due to high" storage costs or regulatory requirements. OTEL: "TODO: document a common approach to record references to externally stored content." SURV p.19: traces "may contain sensitive user data, confidential documents, credentials," and should "support trace minimization, access control, anonymization, retention policies, encryption, and selective disclosure." AUD p.14: "Selective recording, policy-scoped schemas, redaction, encryption, hashed" summaries. GRADE p.9: dependency logs carry task payloads, "so a deployment should minimize" and access-control them. MARS: patterns are monitored "without monitoring the contents of individual conversations". |
| N40 | How long must the record be kept, and when must it be deleted? | A retention period per record class; content retention kept apart from retention of hashes or commitments. | EU Art. 19(1), PDF p.135 (printed 131), providers, and Art. 26(6), PDF p.143 (printed 139), deployers: logs kept "for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months, unless" other law says otherwise, "in particular in Union law on the protection of personal data." HAN p.5: commitment retention, cheap "and durable for years, is decoupled from content retention,". SURV p.19 (retention policies, quoted under N39). |
| N41 | Who may read which parts of the record? | Access scope per record item; the reader's identity; denied reads that do not reveal the item exists; disclosure across organizations under legal process only. | GSM p.12: after the fix, a denied read is "returned as 404 so the row’s existence is not" disclosed. HAN p.5: each trust domain is "to disclose content only under legal process, with inclusion" proofs. AUD p.6: "privacy constrains how evidence can be collected, retained, or redacted, but it does not" replace auditability. My own observation, not a claim from MAST: MAST's sample traces carry plaintext credentials (Fig. 3, p.6). |
| N42 | Can the record be trusted, given that the agents under audit help produce it? | Append-only storage; hash chain; signatures; capture by components outside the agents' control. | AUD p.5, Evidence Integrity levels: 0 none, 1 append-only, 2 hash-chained, 3 signed. HAN p.3: "Witnesses are not agents". CLAIM p.6: SHA-256/HMAC or public-key evidence attestation. |
